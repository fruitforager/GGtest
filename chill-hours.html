Step 1 — Find your station

CIMIS Weather Stations Map

Tap to Explore Bay Area Stations

The stations on this map are part of CIMIS — the California Irrigation Management Information System, a network of over 145 automated weather stations maintained by the California Department of Water Resources. Each station records hourly temperature, humidity, wind, and solar data year-round.

For fruit growers, what matters most is the winter temperature record. CIMIS stations let us count how many hours each winter the temperature stayed in the 32°–45°F range — the sweet spot where fruit trees accumulate "chill hours" during dormancy. Without enough of those cold hours, many trees won't bloom properly or set fruit the following spring.

What These Numbers Mean
Putting Them to Work in Your Orchard

          
          

            

              What is a chill hour?
              
A chill hour is one hour when the temperature stays between 32°F and 45°F. Fruit trees use these cold hours during winter dormancy to reset and bloom properly in spring. Too few chill hours and the tree may not wake up correctly — leading to poor fruit set or no fruit at all.

            

            

              What are growing degree days?
              
Degree days measure how much heat builds up over a season. They are calculated from daily high and low temperatures using a base of 50°F for most fruit trees. Higher degree days generally mean a warmer site that ripens fruit faster — useful for varieties that need a long, warm growing season.

            

            

              Are these numbers from live weather data?
              
The station chill hour and degree day values shown here are illustrative estimates based on historical patterns. Live CIMIS data requires a backend connection to the California Irrigation Management Information System API. The calculator form supports live data once that connection is set up.

            

            

              What should I do with these results?
              
Once you know your station's chill hours, match them to the fruit list to see which varieties are realistic for your yard. Then bring those numbers — and the fruit names — to your local nursery. Staff can help you pick specific low-chill or high-chill cultivars that are well suited to Bay Area conditions. When in doubt, start with a low-chill variety: they are more forgiving in warmer microclimates.

About CIMIS & California Weather Data

          
CIMIS — the California Irrigation Management Information System — is a program run by the California Department of Water Resources (DWR). It operates a statewide network of over 145 automated weather stations that have been collecting detailed climate data since 1982.

          
The system was originally designed to help farmers and water managers make better irrigation decisions, but its rich temperature record has become invaluable for anyone trying to understand California's varied climates — including home orchardists figuring out which fruit trees will thrive in their neighborhood.
